READ BEFORE INTERPRETING

Explicit model assumptions

Same work and service on both sides

All pallet IDs arrive at t = 0. Two forklifts start at staging. Earliest available forklift takes the next pallet, with F1 winning ties. Fixed 3 m/s forklift speed and 1.5 m/s checker walking speed; pickup dwell 4 s, inspection 3 s and dock loading 5 s per pallet. Only staging and checker-station distances change. Check → dock is always 6 m. No percentage savings is authored.

Resources and load accounting

Each forklift remains unavailable until its empty return finishes. The checker walks from their station to inspection, inspects one pallet, then returns before their next task. Forklifts queue for that entire resource cycle. The dock loads only one pallet at a time. Picked means pickup dwell completed. Delivered means dock loading completed. Staging stock + carried pallets + trailer loads always equals the order.

Trailer completion is the last delivery, distinct from fleet return completion. A full-order time delta appears only when both finish within the same window. Distances and actor-seconds include partial tasks clipped at the window. Forklift wait includes inspection queue/wait for checker arrival and dock queue, excluding pickup, inspection and loading service. Walking time sums checker movement. Dock idle is unused dock capacity over the fixed window; more idle after finishing early is available capacity, not automatically inefficiency.

Geometry and limitations

Exact straight routes in metres connect staging → inspection → dock → staging; the checker uses a perpendicular out-and-back route. Forklift symbols are offset ±0.9 m from the shared metric route for legibility; their paths and distances use the route centreline. Stock racks are schematic. Trailer stock uses up to two schematic layers of 24 pallets; no packing or weight-capacity validation is modeled. Queued forklifts remain stationary with explicit queue labels. Resources serialize handling, but travel routes do not model traffic conflict, steering, collisions, acceleration or forklift turning envelopes. The parked trailer identifies the delivery point. The original reversing-truck scenario remains in the facility. These are fictional inputs with no calibrated customer baseline, injury prediction or financial ROI.
