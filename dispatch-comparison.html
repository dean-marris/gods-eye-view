READ BEFORE INTERPRETING

What this trial assumes

A single deterministic seeded trial of a fictional bay. These are simulated exposure proxies, not measured safety outcomes, injury probabilities or validated risk reductions.

Metrics and accounting

Encounter: one continuous episode for a particular person/forklift pair with centre distance strictly below the selected threshold. A clear 0.1-second sample ends an episode. Exposure is the sum of pair-seconds (overlapping pairs count separately). Conflict occupancy is time with at least one person and vehicle footprint in the buffered crossing, counted once per step.

Waiting is active time moving less than 1 cm per 0.1-second step, plus time queued after arrival. Person/vehicle waits sum across actors. Journey distance sums metres actually travelled, including unfinished jobs. Completed means the actor reached its original destination; backlog includes active, arrival-queued and not-yet-arrived jobs at 120 seconds. All metrics cover the same fixed window; changing demand changes both sides.

Movement, footprints and controls

Baseline: direct crossing at x = 0 m, forklift limit 3 m/s, 35% of vehicles yield, no gate. Proposal settings never edit the baseline. A disabled barrier and gate with the baseline speed/yielding settings gives identical runs.

Barrier rails at y = ±4 m channel people to the 2 m opening at x = 8 m. Both directions retain their original destinations. Vehicle lanes are y = ±1.6 m. Conservative circular footprints: people radius 0.35 m; loaded forklifts radius 1.5 m. The conflict area is the crossing strip x = crossing ±1.5 m, y = ±3.5 m, buffered by each actor’s radius.

A compliant gate selects the longest-waiting approaching kind, then holds that phase until its buffered zone clears. Noncompliant actors explicitly ignore this gate; the barrier still channels them. Without a gate, the yielding fraction stops vehicles for approaching people. The same seeded per-actor decisions are reused. Same-lane followers retain footprint clearance; arrivals queue off-scene until that clearance is available and their queue wait is counted.

People limit 1.35 m/s; vehicles use the selected limit. Acceleration is 1.5 / 1 m/s² respectively; braking targets 2 m/s² with a discrete stop-line guard. A newly imposed limit can trigger an immediate stop in this simplified solver, so it does not establish physically feasible braking distances. Rules react to the previous 0.1-second sample, with no extra reaction latency. This is a simple crossing/queue model: no steering, evasive manoeuvres, realistic forklift turning envelope, overtaking, load dynamics or injury model. Low yielding/compliance may allow overlapping cross-traffic footprints; these are unresolved conflicts, not collision physics.

Arrivals are generated at roughly 1.6-second intervals with seeded jitter, alternating person and vehicle jobs and both travel directions. The seed is a reproducibility tool, not an uncertainty estimate. Gate and barrier are independent: a gate can also control the direct crossing.
